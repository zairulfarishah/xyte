Sites · card redesign preview

Site card — glass + tracker

Every detail from today's card is kept, including the photo: location, status, type, report status, relative date, warnings, date range, duration and session, DO number, PIC, crew and all actions. The same five sites appear in every concept: tomorrow, ongoing multi-day, status out of date, report overdue, and a meeting. Hover a card to see the motion.

Card width
G

Glass panel + tracker

E + F COMBINED

E's full-bleed photo and frosted panel, with F's tracker inside the panel. The tracker's caption replaces the separate warning strips: it shows the report state or the problem ("Report overdue · 3d", "Date passed") with a Fix → link, plus the DO number on the right. The date line sits above the tracker, so "when" and "where it's at" read together.

H

Tracker on the photo

E + F VARIANT

Same idea, but the tracker floats on the photo in dark glass, just above the white panel. The photo becomes the "status zone" and the panel holds the job details (name, date, place, people). The panel is shorter, so more of the photo shows, and the tracker stands out more.

H2

Tracker on the photo, decluttered

LESS CROWDED

Same as H, but the panel goes from 6 rows to 3: 1) type, name and client (the title now gets the full width); 2) one line with date · duration · "Yesterday"; 3) the team and the actions on one row. The location moves onto the photo as a glass chip (shortened to the place name; the full address is on hover). "PIC · Person in charge" becomes a ring around the first avatar, so you see the PIC's name plus "+1". The big View button goes: clicking anywhere on the card opens the site (an "Open" hint appears on hover), which leaves small Update / WhatsApp / ⋯ icons.

A

Hero photo

Closest to today's card, with the clutter sorted. Type and status sit on the photo, and the title and client are read over it. Location moves below the photo, so long addresses no longer squeeze the status pill. When and how long are grouped in one block with a mini calendar tile, plus a progress bar for multi-day work. Report and DO become two equal tiles, and warnings show as a full-width strip with a "Fix" action.

B

Inset photo, title on white

The photo sits inset in a rounded frame and only carries status and "when", so it stays clean. The title moves below the photo in dark text, so it's always readable whatever the photo looks like. A type + client line sits on top, and Date / Duration / Report / DO show in a tidy 2×2 table. This is the calmest and most "document" style of the three.

C

Ticket with date tile

Built around the date. A big calendar tile overlaps the photo, so you can scan a row of cards by day at a glance. The details become compact chips, and a perforated "tear line" separates the job from the people and actions. This one has the most personality and works well for a schedule-heavy team.

D

Side photo (wide card)

The photo sits on the left and the details on the right, so each card is shorter and wider. Fewer cards fit across, but more fit down the screen, which suits laptops and scrolling. All facts sit on one line between two hairlines. When the card gets narrow (on phones), it automatically stacks with the photo on top.

E

Full-bleed photo, glass panel

The photo fills the whole card and the details float on a frosted-glass panel at the bottom. It's the most visual of all six and makes the site photo the star. It works best when sites have good cover photos; sites without one fall back to the type gradient, which still looks intentional.

F

Progress tracker

Site status, report status and DO are merged into one journey: Scheduled → On site → Report → Approved (meetings: Scheduled → Held → Done). You see where every job is stuck without reading labels; an overdue report turns its step red. Cancelled or postponed jobs grey out the tracker. This is the most "workflow" style, and the best if the team's main question is "what's next for this job?"
